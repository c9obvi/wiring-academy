Power hookup · battery → fuse → switch → buses

The 12V side: the heavy wires. Work with the battery unplugged and no fuses in the holders until a step says otherwise. Click a step below, and the diagram lights up only the wires for that step.

Two facts that change everything: (1) the red power button on the dash is only a signal. It tells the ESP32 "car on" and carries no real current, so it can't switch power. (2) The BTS7960 has no reverse-polarity protection: battery + on its B− for even a second destroys it. That's why the buses get tested with a meter before any driver is connected.

3 · Your inline fuse holders: how to use one

Each holder comes as one loop of red wire with the fuse socket in the middle. Cut the loop at the far end and you get two leads: one goes toward the battery, the other toward what it protects. Direction doesn't matter to a fuse. Strip about 10 mm and crimp a blue ferrule (14 AWG) on each end before it goes into a Wago or a screw terminal.

You'll use 2 holders: one for the 20A main fuse and one for the steering driver's 10A fuse. The buck has its own fuse slot, so it doesn't need one.

5 · The main switch

It goes after the 20A fuse, so the fuse also protects the switch's wires. Plain 2-wire toggle: one lead from the fuse, the other to the +12V Wago. Toggle with a light (3 wires): the third, usually black, goes to a spare port on the GND Wago. Send me a photo and I'll confirm which wire is which.

Why you need it: the ESP32 never sleeps (Wi-Fi), so the system draws about 0.1A all the time. That runs a 12V ride-on battery flat in a few days, and a fully flattened lead-acid battery gets damaged. No switch yet? Leave it out, wire the 20A fuse straight to Wago+ port 1, and unplug the big white plug after every ride.

6 · The big white plug

Keep the plug: it's your battery disconnect. On this car its pink/red wire is + (after the stock breaker) and its brown wire is −: ground runs from battery − through the charge port first, which cuts it while charging. When the old board comes out, cut its wires at the board end, so the car-side half of the plug keeps long red and black leads. Those leads become your new power input. The battery half and the charge port stay stock.

Plug it in, then put the meter on the car-side leads: red probe on the pink/red pin, black probe on the brown pin: +12.x V. A minus sign means it's the other way round. Stop and tell me. Bonus check: push the charger's plug into the charge port (charger not in the wall). If the reading drops to 0 V, that's the charging safety switch, and it's working.
