Words you'll see, in plain English
+ / −Positive and negative (ground). Current flows out of +, through whatever it powers, and back to −. If + touches − directly, that's a short: huge current, heat, sparks. The fuse exists for that moment.
FuseA deliberately weak link. Too much current melts its little metal strip and the circuit stops, before a wire gets hot. A 20A fuse lets up to 20 amps through. A blown fuse is a 30-cent part; replace it with the same number.
BusOne place where many wires share the same thing. Your "+ bus" is a 5-port Wago: everything plugged into it is connected to + together.
WagoThe orange-lever connectors. Lever up, push the wire in all the way, lever down. Every port in one Wago is connected to every other port in it.
FerruleThe little metal tube you crimp on the end of a stripped wire, so the strands can't fray and every strand gets clamped. Blue = 14 AWG, red = 18 AWG, white = thin wire.
AWGWire thickness. Smaller number = thicker wire. 14 AWG is your thick red/black, 18 AWG the thinner one.
B+ / B− / M+ / M−The big screw terminals on the BTS7960 motor driver. B = battery in, M = motor out.
BuckYour 12V → 5V converter. It feeds the ESP32 through a USB cable, like a car phone charger.
