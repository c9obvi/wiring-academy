3 · Wago B (3-port, logic ground)

Every wire gets a white ferrule (E0508). The resistor leg shares port 2 with the driver GND wire: twist them together, one ferrule.

4 · The Y-splice (one solder joint: ties both enables to D4 and adds the 10k safety resistor)

Why the 10k resistor? While the ESP32 boots, its pins float for about half a second. The resistor holds R_EN and L_EN at ground, so the driver stays off until the firmware says go. With no motor power connected you could skip it today, but it must be in before battery day.

5 · Do it in this order

3V3 and GND are next-door neighbours on the left column (pins 1 and 2). A jumper one pin off shorts the board's power and it drops off Wi-Fi, which is exactly what bit us before. Check them twice.
